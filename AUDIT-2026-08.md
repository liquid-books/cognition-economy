# The Cognition Economy — Manuscript Audit (August 2026)

Goal: make the book read true in 2030. The frameworks (four moats, six disciplines, two mental models, specialist mindset, light cone, tissue architecture) hold. The rot is concentrated in: (1) the three-track exercise blocks pasted into 13 chapters (Antigravity 2.0, CMD+E, doc URLs, click paths — ~1/3 of each chapter, ~100% perishable); (2) calendar dates in case studies; (3) model versions and prices in body text; (4) vendor capability/policy claims stated as fact, several already false.

## Seven book-wide moves (apply everywhere)

1. **Companion pages.** Move all Track B and Track C content (Antigravity/Claude Code/Agent Manager steps, CMD+E shortcuts, /agents /mcp /goal commands, config snippets, doc URLs) to a companion page reference: `drlee.io/chNN`. Keep Track A in print (it's "paste this prompt into a chat" — evergreen). Replace the three-track admonition with one paragraph describing the three surface types (a chat assistant; a terminal agent inside an IDE; an agent-orchestration workspace) plus the pointer. Date-stamp companion pages, never the book.
2. **Models by role, not name/version.** "The flagship reasoning tier," "the mid-tier daily driver," "the fast/cheap tier." Keep ONE dated roster (the lineup boxes in 1.3) as a visually set-off "Model Roster, as of [edition]" sidebar with the drlee.io pointer. Rename the Opus-Plus-Sonnet Pattern (Ch 6) to the **Architect-and-Builder Pattern**; mention Opus/Sonnet once in parentheses.
3. **Case studies get sequence, not calendar.** "In early 2024" → "Eighteen months into the firm's AI rollout." "By December 2024" → "Six months after go-live." "March 4, 2026" → "at the end of his first quarter." Round invented dollar figures ("$3.1-billion, 31 branches, 410,000 members" → "a $3-billion credit union with about thirty branches"). Add once in front matter: *The firms and people in the case studies are composites; dates place them in the early years of the transition.*
4. **Prices → orders of magnitude; capability numbers → tiers.** "$20/month" → "the price of a lunch" / "a consumer subscription" (one literal figure allowed in the roster sidebar). Token prices → ratios ("output usually costs 3–5× input") and directions ("prices have fallen roughly an order of magnitude every couple of years"). Context windows → two tiers ("a few hundred thousand tokens — a long novel; a million or more — a trilogy").
5. **Vendor claims → checklists the reader can run.** Replace assertions ("Claude cannot send email," "it does not remember you," "Telegram, Discord and iMessage are supported") with verifiable questions ("check what the permission screen says it can write"; "ask your assistant what it already knows about you"; "pull the vendor's current Data Processing Addendum"). Add a short **"Is this still true?" sidebar** at the end of every tool-heavy chapter.
6. **Widen the "Note on Model Names"** (in How to Read) into "A Note on Names, Numbers, and Buttons" — the book-wide perishability policy. Name the four kinds of facts that expire (model versions; prices and plan names; capability numbers; menu paths/button labels/shortcuts); say every one was accurate at press time and will change; give the single companion URL. Edition stamp on the copyright page ("Tool references current as of Fall 2026"). Chapters then stop apologizing in-line ("or the equivalent in your version," "these evolve quickly," "as of this writing" — cut these).
7. **Audit figures.** Images that bake in dates/versions: ch00-7 AI Revolution timeline ("2017–2025"), ch01-12 model comparison matrix (named versions), ch06 Opus/Sonnet diagram, ch15 moat-collapse chart ("2022–2027"), ch14 ZDR comparison. Relabel with relative eras ("Flagship / Balanced / Fast") or make the fixed window explicit.
