# Who This Book Is For

This book was written for **business people**. That category is broad on purpose.

**Specifically, it was written for:**

- The **undergraduate** who wants to graduate already operating at a professional level — not waiting for a job to teach them what school didn't
- The **MBA student** who knows that "AI fluency" is becoming a table-stakes skill and wants the real version, not the buzzword version
- The **operator** — manager, director, VP — being asked to "do something with AI" and needing a framework that actually holds up
- The **founder** who has a $20/month Claude subscription and a vision but doesn't yet know how to make the two meet
- The **individual contributor** who wants to multiply their output without multiplying their hours
- The **consultant, analyst, marketer, finance professional** who works primarily in language and logic — exactly the kind of work AI augments most dramatically

If you are trying to build things, lead teams, serve customers, or create value — this book is for you.

---

**What you do NOT need**

- A computer science degree
- Any programming experience
- Math beyond what you use to read a spreadsheet
- Prior experience with AI tools (though it doesn't hurt)
- Any specific software installed before Chapter 1 (setup is covered in the next section)

The technical details in this book are explained in plain language before they are explained in technical terms. If a technical term appears, it is defined the first time you see it.

---

**What mindset IS required**

You need to be willing to **do the exercises**. Every chapter ends with one. They are not optional enrichment — they are the course. Reading about AI without using it is like reading about swimming. The concepts make sense, but you still can't swim.

You need to be comfortable with **partial understanding**. AI systems are probabilistic and sometimes counterintuitive. The goal is not to understand everything perfectly before you act. The goal is to act, observe, and refine.

You need a **builder's orientation** — the instinct to ask "what could I make with this?" rather than "what does this do?" Those are different questions and they lead to very different outcomes.

If that sounds like you, keep reading.
