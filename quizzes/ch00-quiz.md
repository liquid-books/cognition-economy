## Question 10

## Answer Key

1. **B** — The chapter's central framing is that the Industrial Revolution was a *business model reset*, not just a technology event. The key shift was muscle becoming a rentable commodity, which reorganized how economic advantage was created.

2. **B** — Thomas added machines to his existing structure (adoption); George tore out the old floor plan and redesigned everything around the new throughput (reorganization). The chapter uses this contrast to illustrate why redesign, not adoption, produces durable advantage.

3. **C** — Before industrialization, all output depended on biological energy — human backs, animal legs, wind, and water. Scarcity of physical effort was the primary economic constraint.

4. **C** — The four leverage points named are Steam, Steel, Railroads, and Factories. Electricity is not among them; it was a later development and belongs to a subsequent era.

5. **B** — The AI Revolution commoditizes cognitive processing (reading, summarizing, classifying, drafting). As a result, the new bottleneck shifts from "can we process the information?" to "do we know what we're trying to do with it?" — i.e., disciplined strategic thinking.

6. **C** — The analogy illustrates that AI adoption will quickly become a baseline, not a differentiator — just as "having electricity" stopped being a competitive edge once every factory had it. The strategic advantage comes from how you *reorganize around* AI, not simply from using it.

7. **C** — The Builder's Question is an *architecture* question, not a productivity question. It asks whether work can be redesigned so that AI makes it *structurally better*, not just faster.

8. **C** — Carlos uses AI as a search engine (queries → facts). Priya uses it as a reasoning partner by providing a rich context file and asking the AI to challenge assumptions and surface blind spots. The chapter's lesson: the model responds to *disciplined input* with dramatically better output.

9. **C** — The chapter explicitly defines *judgment* as requiring experience, values, relationships, and accountability, and *processing* as the cognitive work of reading, summarizing, classifying, drafting, and comparing. Routing processing to AI while elevating human judgment is the core of the redesign framework.

10. **B** — This is the book's central thesis: the pattern from the Industrial Revolution is repeating. Cognition is now rentable. The winners will be those who ask the Builder's Question and redesign their operations — not those who merely adopt tools and expect a lift.
